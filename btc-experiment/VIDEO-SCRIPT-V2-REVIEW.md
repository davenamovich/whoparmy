# The BTC Experiment — Video Script v2 Review

## 1. LOCKS COMPLIANCE

- **No earnings promises or implied income — PASS, with one wording caution.** The script explicitly says, “Honestly? We don't know. That's why it's called an experiment and not a promise,” and the end card says, “No earnings are guaranteed.” “Their affiliate program pays in crypto” describes the payout form, not an amount or outcome. “Get your agent” is a call to action, not an earnings claim.
- **No matrix/multi-level math — PASS.** No matrix, levels, recruitment math, percentages, or payout calculations appear.
- **DH Media’s $10 is their price, paid to them, optional — FAIL.** The required attribution is present, but “DH Media's own starter pack” invents a program/package term not in the supplied facts. Offending line: “DH Media's own starter pack is ten dollars — their price, paid to them, optional.”
- **No invented DH Media program terms — FAIL.** “Starter pack” is an invented DH Media program term. “The works” and “social media” are broad paraphrases; the supplied facts support the named tools, but not a generic package called a starter pack.
- **Public-BTC-addresses-only; never keys/seed phrases — PASS, narrowly.** The script says, “Your Bitcoin addresses? Public ones only. Barry will never ask for your keys.” It does not mention seed phrases or request sensitive credentials. For maximum precision, “public Bitcoin addresses only” would be cleaner.
- **Nothing guarantees the experiment succeeds — PASS.** “Honestly? We don't know,” “If the report says zero, Barry says zero,” “let the numbers say what they say,” and the end-card disclaimer keep the honesty frame intact. The phrase “The prize… is an employee” is a trust/positioning concern, not a success guarantee.

## 2. FACT CHECK

- **Correct:** “Twenty-six briefcases” matches the game fact. The game should be named exactly “BTC or No BTC”; the script does that.
- **Correct:** “Meet BTC Barry” matches the agent persona.
- **Correct:** “Barry is free — he comes with Whop Army when you save your DH Media link” matches the supplied unlock condition, though “comes with Whop Army” is less exact than “unlocks free in Whop Army.”
- **Unsupported / needs correction:** “Barry researches, makes the memes, works the traffic, and files the report.” The supplied fact is narrower: Barry works a **daily traffic pool of 10 rotator slots**. Research, meme-making, and report-filing are not among the facts supplied for this review.
- **Missing specificity:** The script says Barry “works the traffic” but never states the verified “daily traffic pool of 10 rotator slots.” That is a material product fact worth using instead.
- **Supported:** DH Media is described as a business tools suite, and the script names websites, booking, and automations. “Social media” is a loose label for the supplied Social Planner; “the works” is vague. The fact set also includes a funnel builder, email, and affiliate manager, none of which are named.
- **Supported, but tighten wording:** “Their affiliate program pays in crypto” is consistent with “crypto payouts.” “Bitcoin-paying opportunity” is understandable shorthand, but “crypto-payout opportunity” is more exact.
- **Unsupported / invented:** “DH Media's own starter pack” is not in the supplied DH Media facts.
- **Correct:** The end-card URL is exactly `whop.plunderandriffle.com/bridge`.

## 3. HUMOR

- **Lands for Joe:** The encyclopedia cold open, “Nobody wanted those either. At first”; the rotary-phone comparison; “I use that word carefully”; and “a raccoon in a suit” are clear, visual, old-school pitchman jokes.
- **Mostly lands:** “The prize… is an employee” is a good bait-and-switch, but it also makes the offer sound like a guaranteed labor substitute. Keep only if the production wants that intentionally comic, slightly exaggerated framing.
- **Falls toward crypto-bro / generic tech copy:** “Bitcoin doesn't sleep,” “Bitcoin-paying opportunity,” and “the works.” They are familiar crypto/automation sales language rather than Joe-specific jokes.
- **Trust risk:** “Barry researches, makes the memes, works the traffic, and files the report” makes Barry sound omnipotent before the experiment has demonstrated anything. It weakens the honest “we don’t know” setup.
- **Strong trust line:** “If the report says zero, Barry says zero” is the best honesty joke in the script and should remain, provided the report is actually part of the product behavior.

## 4. CRAFT

- **Runtime:** The six quoted scene lines contain about **262 spoken words** (excluding stage directions, metadata, and the end card). That is about **93 seconds at 170 wpm**, **98 seconds at 160 wpm**, and **105 seconds at 150 wpm**. With pitchman pauses and laugh beats, the practical runtime is likely about **100–110 seconds**, not 95. The script’s “~235 words” note is inaccurate.
- **Scene producibility:** The six scenes are producible with one actor. Props are clearly named: briefcases, phone, desk/tools, empty chair or glowing briefcase, fingers, and coin. The “whole toolbox” desk needs a defined prop/UI treatment so it does not become generic filler.
- **Sayability/lip-sync:** The repeated short “Bitcoin doesn’t…” sentences are easy to perform. The densest lines are the DH Media sentence and the “researches, makes the memes, works the traffic, and files the report” list; both are rapid, clause-heavy, and likely to need extra takes. “DH Media's own starter pack is ten dollars — their price, paid to them, optional” is especially unnatural in the mouth because of the stacked qualification.
- **Visual continuity:** The empty chair / glowing briefcase can sell Barry without adding a second actor. The end card should hold long enough to read the URL and disclaimer, which pushes total duration beyond the spoken estimate.

## 5. VERDICT

**Ship with named line fixes.** The honesty frame and security lock are strong, but the script should not go to final production verbatim while it contains an invented “starter pack” and unsupported Barry capabilities.

- Replace: “The job is DH Media — a whole toolbox for a business: websites, booking, automations, social media, the works.”
  With: “The job is DH Media — a toolbox for a business: a website and funnel builder, booking, automations, email, Social Planner, and an affiliate manager.”
- Replace: “Barry researches, makes the memes, works the traffic, and files the report.”
  With: “Barry works a daily traffic pool of ten rotator slots.”
- Replace: “DH Media's own starter pack is ten dollars — their price, paid to them, optional.”
  With: “DH Media is optional; if you choose it, it’s ten dollars paid to DH Media — their price.”
- Replace: “And their affiliate program pays in crypto.”
  With: “And their affiliate program offers crypto payouts.”
- Replace the runtime note “~235 words ≈ 95s” with “~262 spoken words; roughly 100–110s with natural pitchman pauses, before the end-card hold.”
